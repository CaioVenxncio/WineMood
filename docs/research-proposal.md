# WineMood: Research Proposal

**Version:** 2.0 (Phase 2, 2026-10-12). It supersedes the Phase 1 draft.
**Status:** methodology fixed; **no models have been trained and no
performance results exist.** The dataset statistics quoted here are
generated by code and published in [`data-report.md`](data-report.md).

## 1. Problem statement

Professional wine reviews combine a short tasting note with a numeric score
assigned by the same critic. WineMood asks:

> **To what extent can a critic's numeric score be predicted from the text of
> their tasting note, and which properties of the text carry that information
> beyond trivial cues such as length and author identity?**

This is a text-regression problem with an ordinal interpretation. It is **not**
sentiment analysis. The scores are the critic's own quality ratings, not
annotations of expressed opinion (see
[`problem-formulation.md`](problem-formulation.md)). The closest established
framing is rating inference from review text (Pang & Lee, 2005).

## 2. Motivation and related work

Tasting notes are a specialised register. They are dense with sensory
vocabulary and evaluative hedges, and are rarely overtly negative. Lefever et
al. (2018) showed that wine-review text predicts properties such as colour,
grape variety and country of origin. They also analysed how review length and
word length relate to price and score. WineMood focuses on the score itself
and on separating textual signal from shortcuts. That emphasis follows recent
work showing that evaluation choices can change conclusions:
- standard versus random splits (Gorman & Bedrick, 2019; Søgaard et al., 2021);
- train–test duplication (Lee et al., 2022);
- data leakage more broadly (Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023).

## 3. Research questions

- **RQ1 (Predictability).** Does tasting-note text predict the critic's score
  better than trivial baselines (mean, review length)?
- **RQ2 (Model capacity).** Do sequence models (LSTM; optionally a pretrained
  Transformer) improve on a regularised bag-of-words model in this register?
- **RQ3 (Author confound).** How much predictive signal survives when the test
  tasters are unseen during training?
- **RQ4 (Linguistic carriers).** Which lexical features are associated with
  higher scores, and are they shortcuts (length, drinking-window years) or
  evaluative descriptors?
- **RQ5 (Preprocessing).** Does preserving negations change performance?

## 4. Hypotheses

The operational tests and decision rules are pre-registered in
[`experimental-protocol.md`](experimental-protocol.md) §9.

- **H1:** TF-IDF + Ridge achieves lower test MAE than both the mean predictor
  and a length-only model.
- **H2:** A sequence model improves MAE over TF-IDF + Ridge by more than
  seed-to-seed variation. The null outcome is informative and will be
  reported as such.
- **H3:** MAE is higher on a taster-held-out split than on a size-matched
  random split of the same rows.
- **H4 (exploratory):** masking 4-digit numbers (drinking windows) changes
  performance measurably.
- **H5:** keeping negation tokens does not reduce performance.

## 5. Dataset

**Source.** *Wine Reviews* (Kaggle, `zynicide/wine-reviews`, version 4, last
updated 2017-11-27, creator "zackthoutt"), file `winemag-data-130k-v2.csv`.
The reviews were scraped from WineEnthusiast. The creator states the scrape was
repeated on 2017-11-22 to add `title`, `taster_name` and the taster's Twitter
handle. Pinned SHA-256: `52af2643c8ac29f010f0cc629dfbdda1c74aa0f332d11762af9ef3de4e567ac9`.
**Licence:** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (from Kaggle dataset metadata).

**Verified properties** (from [`data-report.md`](data-report.md))

- 129,971 rows and 14 columns.
- No missing descriptions or scores.
- 10,016 exact duplicate descriptions, and 23 duplicate groups with
  conflicting scores.
- 2 texts with encoding damage ("mojibake"). They are reported, not altered.
- Scores are integers from 80 to 100: mean 88.44, SD 3.09 after deduplication.
- 19 named tasters, with 24,912 deduplicated reviews lacking a taster.
- 43 countries. The US accounts for 50,447 deduplicated reviews.

**Sampling limitations**

- Data from a single publication, scraped in 2017.
- English only.
- Scores are censored below 80.
- A few critics dominate: the largest taster wrote 19.6 % of reviews.
- Wines are submitted to the publication rather than sampled, so the corpus
  reflects the publication's coverage, not the wine market.

## 6. Labelling methodology

All targets are deterministic, versioned functions of `points`
(`src/winemood/data/labels.py`):

- `points_regression_v1`: the integer score. **This is the primary target.**
- `we_category_v1`: Wine Enthusiast's published categories.
  `we_category_merged_v1` is the same scheme with Classic (0.11 %) merged into
  Superb, using a rule declared in advance and applied to the training split.
- `points_binary_90_v1`: ≥ 90 vs < 90.
- `points_band_v0`: the deprecated legacy rule, kept for history only. Its
  "negative" class is empty.

Validation rejects missing, non-integer and out-of-range scores. Empty classes
are reported automatically. No label is described as sentiment.

## 7. Experimental design and evaluation

This section summarises [`experimental-protocol.md`](experimental-protocol.md).

- **Splits:** deduplicated, near-duplicate- and wine-identity-grouped,
  stratified 70/10/20, with a taster-held-out split for RQ3.
- **Rules:** every learned transformation is fitted on train only, and the
  test set is used once per final model.
- **Models:**
  - baselines: dummy, length-only, metadata-only, TF-IDF + Ridge/Logistic
    Regression;
  - an Embedding + LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber, 1997);
  - a pretrained Transformer (Devlin et al., 2019), conditional on compute.
- **Metrics:**
  - regression: MAE (primary), RMSE, R², and residual analysis;
  - classification: macro-F1, weighted-F1, balanced accuracy, per-class
    precision and recall, confusion matrices, and quadratic-weighted κ for
    the ordinal target.
- **Statistics:** paired bootstrap confidence intervals (Dror et al., 2018)
  and 5 seeds for neural models.
- **Tooling:** scikit-learn (Pedregosa et al., 2011) for baselines and metrics.

## 8. Ethical and legal considerations

- **Licence.** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 allows **non-commercial** use with
  attribution, and requires derivatives to be shared under the same licence.
  Consequences:
  - The raw data is not redistributed in this repository.
  - Derived artifacts (trained models, split manifests containing review
    IDs) are treated as derivatives.
  - No commercial deployment is planned. The original README's "ready for
    commercial integration" claim was removed in Phase 1.
- **Real people.** Tasters are named public critics. Reports use anonymised
  aggregates, such as reviews per taster or the range of mean scores. They do
  not rank or characterise named individuals. Twitter handles are never
  analysed or published.
- **Interpretation.** The models predict a critic's rating, not wine quality
  or consumer preference. Associations between words and scores are not
  causal.
- **Documentation.** The data documentation follows the spirit of datasheets
  for datasets (Gebru et al., 2021): provenance, composition, collection and
  intended uses are recorded in `data/README.md` and this proposal.

## 9. Threats to validity

- **Construct validity.** A score reflects one critic's judgement on one day.
  Category thresholds are the publisher's convention, not natural kinds.
- **Internal validity.** Measured shortcuts include:
  - review length (Spearman ρ = 0.51 with score on the training split);
  - drinking-window years, which appear among the top score-associated terms;
  - taster identity (mean score per taster ranges from 85.78 to 90.63).

  The protocol controls for these with baselines, ablations and a held-out
  taster split. Leakage via duplicates and same-wine reviews is handled by
  grouping.
- **External validity.** The data covers one publication, English, and
  2017-era reviews. It does not transfer to consumer reviews without
  evidence.
- **Statistical conclusion validity.** The protocol uses paired bootstrap
  confidence intervals, multiple seeds for neural models, and pre-registered
  comparisons.
- **Reliability.** The 23 conflicting-duplicate groups show that the target
  itself carries noise.

## 10. Future directions

- **Genuine sentiment:** a human-annotated polarity subset with written
  guidelines and inter-annotator agreement, as a separate task (Option C).
- **Per-taster calibration:** does a "92" mean the same thing across critics?
- **Transfer:** test on another publication's reviews, if a legally usable
  corpus exists.
- **Interpretability:** compare linear coefficients with attribution methods
  for the neural models.
